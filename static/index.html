Guide

The Game of Life

Conway's Life is a two-state cellular automaton on an infinite grid. Each cell looks at its 8 Moore-neighborhood neighbors and follows two rules: birth (a dead cell comes alive when it has exactly the right number of alive neighbors) and survival (an alive cell stays alive with the right neighbor count).

Classical Life uses B3/S23: born with 3 neighbors, survives with 2 or 3. This is Turing-complete — every computation possible in principle expressible as a pattern of living cells.

Life-like rules (B/S notation)

Life-like rules share Conway's structure (two states, Moore neighborhood, outer totalistic) but vary the neighbor counts that trigger birth and survival. Named rules in the dropdown:

  • HighLife (B36/S23) — like Life but includes a self-replicator.
  • Seeds (B2/S) — every live cell dies; explosive growth from any seed.
  • Day & Night — symmetric under state inversion.
  • Life Without Death — once born, never dies; grows forever.
  • Maze / Mazectric — stabilizes into maze-like patterns.

Toggle any checkbox to leave a named rule and explore custom regimes.

Famous patterns

Spaceships (glider, LWSS, MWSS, HWSS) translate across the grid forever. Oscillators (blinker, toad, pulsar, pentadecathlon) repeat with periods 2, 2, 3, 15. Still lifes (block, beehive, loaf, boat) are fixed points. Guns emit spaceships periodically — the Gosper glider gun fires every 30 generations. Methuselahs (R-pentomino, acorn, diehard) are tiny seeds that evolve for hundreds of generations before stabilizing.

Controls

Mouse. In Brush mode: left-drag paints alive, shift+drag or right-drag erases. In Stamp mode: single-click drops the active stamp, right-click clears its footprint.

Keyboard. P pause · M step · R reset scene · C clear · B toggle boundary · [ / ] brush radius.

Rendering. The colormap maps cell age to a gradient. Fresh births glow green, long-lived stable structures turn white, and dying cells leave a fading comet tail.
